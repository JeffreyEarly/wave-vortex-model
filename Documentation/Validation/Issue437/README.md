# T6 named APV-coordinate damping qualification

This increment defines and measures `WVThermalAPVDamping`, the explicit candidate closure described by [issue 437](https://github.com/JeffreyEarly/wave-vortex-model/issues/437). It preserves the complete thermal state and freezes an independently constructed six-coordinate APV diagnostic band with both endpoints. It is not an equivalent full-state replacement for the APV transform and is not a selected campaign closure. The [milestone validation report](../BalancedDiffusionMilestone2.md) records the common evolution adapter, controller regressions and forcing-owned all-stage stability tests separately.

## Equation and comparison contract

At each physical horizontal radius, `P` projects the complete thermal state into six APV and two residual endpoint coordinates. `L` is the positive-physical-energy-minimum right inverse, with `P*L=I`. The closure is `Umax*(h(k)*I + L*D*P)`, in s-1, where `h(k)` is the existing horizontal wavenumber filter per speed and `D` contains the frozen APV vertical rates per speed followed by two zeros. Horizontal damping acts on every thermal direction. The vertical complement `(I-L*P)` and the independent MDA family are unchanged by vertical damping. No thermal-eigenvector index is used as an APV ordinal index.

Physical endpoint tendencies include the response of damped APV content. They are not zero merely because the residual endpoint-coordinate entries of `D` are zero. The manufactured comparison uses the same resolved thermal state and physical speed for the named closure and the actual `WVAdaptiveDamping` law applied to its diagnosed APV coordinates. A nonzero high-wavenumber pressure component makes the horizontal comparison nontrivial.

The coordinate equality is deliberately narrower than a full physical-state equality. The retained `mapped-comparison.csv` measures APV coordinate errors below 3.7e-13 and endpoint tendency errors below 1.2e-13 relative. The reconstructed full QGPV tendencies differ by approximately 1.08% for horizontal action and 0.096% for vertical action in the selected state. Horizontal action retains and damps content absent from the smaller APV reconstruction; the vertical minimum-energy lift is also a distinct reconstruction. The vertical complement test passes independently. This is a changed closure with its own persisted configuration.

The positive-energy minimum lift does not make unequal diagonal APV rates physically dissipative. In energy coordinates `B=R*A/R`, physical work is controlled by the Hermitian part `(B+B')/2`, which can have positive eigenvalues. [The independent energy audit](energy-audit.csv) measures these extrema and confirms the forcing's norm bound covers the actual nonnormal action. The controller uses an induced operator norm, not an eigenvalue maximum. Physical-energy injection is reported explicitly, not classified as dissipation. See the [T7 audit and integrated budgets](../Issue438/README.md) for constant and exponential controls, including an ordinary exponential state with positive integrated vertical work.

## Matched evolved-state controls

A fresh deterministic mixed pressure field is evolved without damping or diffusion for 100000 s before comparison. The precursor's physical energy-norm change is 36.32%, so continuation begins from an evolved state rather than repeating the initial manufactured pattern. It is a short developed-flow control, not a claim of equilibrated turbulence.

The common precursor is rescaled to maximum speeds 0.001, 0.01 and 0.1 m/s (1x, 10x and 100x). Each pair uses identical initial coefficients, a 500 km square domain, 1 km depth, `N2=1e-4*exp(2*z/1300)`, latitude 24 degrees, 12 by 12 horizontal grid, 129 depth samples, 17 thermal directions, four signed MDA modes, scalar diffusivity 0.01 m2/s and quadratic drag `Cd=1e-3`. The fixed APV band has six modes and default signed endpoint weights; cutoff fraction is 0.5. Each undamped/closure pair advances for 20000 s with maximum steps 200, 100 and 50 s.

`developed-comparison.csv`, `developed-bias-*.csv` and `developed-*.csv` retain the endpoint differences, time controls, direct and integrated horizontal/vertical work, full diffusion feedback, positive high-APV diffusion input and physical regime measurements. Budget sampling is 5 s through 100 s, 50 s through 1000 s and 250 s afterward, explicitly retaining rapid initial adjustment. The independent inventory/power contraction and sampling refinement were qualified by T7; this study uses the same RHS process breakdown and quadratic metrics.

The finest time-control differences are below 7.1e-14 relative, within a predeclared 1e-5 gate and a roundoff-aware convergence check. The 100x closure changes the final fields by at most 0.2845% relative, with surface and bottom displacement RMS differences 5.49 mm and 16.07 mm. Its physical energy work is negative horizontally and positive vertically. Positive diffusion production in the upper two coordinates of the frozen six-mode APV band is measurable even while total physical enstrophy decreases. The closure changes that diffusion input through its changed full state; the measured work is not estimated by a coefficient-norm filter alone.

At 100x, the final maximum displacement/depth ratio is approximately 0.0285 and maximum relative vorticity divided by `|f|` is approximately 0.0421. These bounds describe this short control's QG regime; they do not establish long-time stability. The largest observed accepted damping number is 2.49e-4 in the fine controls. Separate controller tests exercise the limiting and rejected-stage cases.

Tail meanings are explicit. The horizontal tail is a full physical QGPV variance fraction at radii at least 0.8 of the retained maximum. The APV tail is the upper two coordinates' variance divided by variance within the fixed six-coordinate diagnostic band, not total thermal enstrophy. The polynomial tail is the physical QGPV RMS of the last 20% of pressure polynomial degrees divided by total QGPV RMS; it is a truncation residual, not an additive energy partition or an APV modal spectrum. A small damped tail is not an accuracy claim.

## Bounded target seasonal comparison

The seasonal control uses the actual target's 500 km square, 4 km depth, latitude 24 degrees, `N2=(5.2e-3)^2*exp(2*z/1300)`, scalar diffusivity 1e-5 m2/s, both active endpoints and annual meridional mode-5 source. It retains 257 thermal directions, four MDA modes, an 18 by 18 horizontal grid, 385 native depth samples and 1029 construction quadrature points. Thermal nonlinear product qualification is preserved, although this comparison disables advection and drag to isolate source, insulating diffusion and the named closure. For this single meridional mode the advective Jacobian vanishes. It is a short bias experiment, not a numerical continuation of a historical checkpoint or a fresh seeded campaign.

The initial state is the exact same-space forced diffusion response after 2.5 years from zero. Both cases advance for 1000000 s (11.57 days), with the continued exact undamped response as the physical reference. This reuses the literature's seasonal-bias design while avoiding a long numerical spinup. The M10 and M100 controls each use four independent timestep refinements. The first configured maximum is 86400 s; subsequent maxima halve the actual accepted maximum, so an active closure norm cap cannot masquerade as timestep refinement. Every accepted step is retained in `seasonal-accepted-steps-*.csv`.

The APV band remains six modes with the default signed weights `g0=-integral(N2 dz)` and `gd=+integral(N2 dz)`. Its scientific basis is constructed at 129 native samples, then the provider's own `discreteTransform` resamples that same fixed basis at 513, 1025 and 2049 WKB samples. No alternative approximate projection is introduced. The online closure freezes the 2049-sample result. Relative differences from 513/1025 to 2049 are respectively 3.88e-12/1.12e-12 for the energy-scaled projection and 9.77e-11/5.77e-11 for the induced energy-norm action. This separates retained APV band size, APV projection sampling and thermal dimension.

The [final seasonal table](seasonal-comparison.csv) measures a substantial combined-closure bias in this grid configuration:

The M100 bottom reference is only 4.42895 mm RMS, so its large percentage must be read alongside the absolute difference. Undamped propagation agrees exactly with the same-space analytic response. The final time-control differences are at most 1.35e-9 relative for M10 and 6.20e-11 for M100, far smaller than the measured bias. Fine accepted maximum steps are 4377.4751 and 1548.2458 s, respectively. Coarse runs reach an accepted damping number of 1, exercising the explicit norm restriction; the four actual step sequences remain distinct. Finest integration-only times are 5.47 and 14.69 s on the shared host, excluding construction, maps and diagnostics.

Two earlier refinement failures are retained. `seasonal-initial-nonindependent-control.csv` records the M10 failure when configured 86400/43200 s runs were both capped to the same accepted sequence. Its coarse difference was exactly zero, so it provided no independent refinement evidence. `seasonal-initial-plateau-control.csv` records the M100 ratio failure after genuine refinement: absolute differences were already approximately 1.5e-9 m at the surface and a few 1e-12 m at the bottom. The final fourth control retains all successive differences and applies the unchanged 1e-5 relative gate. A ratio need not demonstrate asymptotic order when both final differences also lie below the integrator's existing physical floors: QGPV 1e-13 s-1, buoyancy 1e-11 m/s2, speed 1e-8 m/s and each endpoint 1e-8 m. `seasonal-time-control-*.csv` records those floors, all successive differences and a standard `gamma_n*|F|*|a|` dot-product bound. That bound applies only to final physical contractions; it is not a bound on accumulated trajectory error. No convergence order is claimed below the floors.

The 18 by 18 grid puts the forced mode 5 inside the horizontal filter transition. On the historical 64 by 64 grid the same mode is below cutoff. The measured bias therefore concerns this explicit grid and candidate configuration; it does not predict the campaign's closure bias, and this coarse horizontal configuration is not recommended for a seasonal campaign. The six-mode diagnostic band is also distinct from richer historical APV diagnostics. T10 must select and qualify its actual resolution and closure together.

## Sampling, timing and reproducibility

The authoring tool is `tools/qualifyThermalDamping.m`. It supports separate `mapped`, `developed`, `seasonal` and `timing` sections so a successful section need not be repeated after an unrelated correction. With the clean setup documented in [T7](../Issue438/README.md):

```matlab
results = qualifyThermalDamping('Documentation/Validation/Issue437');
```

The installed provider is InternalModes v2.0.0-beta.4, revision `f2ce3c143744ae00fbb25bd9d7b8c73fb358ca51`; older sibling checkouts are excluded after `configureCIEnvironment`, and unique symbol resolution is asserted. Dependencies are Distributions 2.0.0, SplineCore 2.2.0, chebfun 5.7.0, NetCDF 1.0.2 and ClassAnnotations 1.2.1. Runs use MATLAB R2026a Update 4, double precision, `maca64`, and two computational threads. Scientific arrays and experiment snapshots are not modified.

The cached online closure measurement uses three warm-up calls and 15 timed calls with physical speed already available, on 17 thermal directions, six APV modes, 24 nonzero Fourier columns and nine radius pages. Median time is approximately 0.67 ms on this shared host. It includes actual horizontal and vertical coefficient actions, but excludes scientific construction, physical reconstruction, plotting, I/O and the timestep controller. It is not a throughput benchmark or performance regression measurement. `online-cost.csv` retains minimum/median/maximum times and the exact work shape.

`online-cost-target.csv` repeats that narrowly scoped measurement on the target seasonal state: 257 thermal directions, six APV modes, 56 nonzero Fourier columns, 18 radius pages and two threads. Median action time is 0.575 ms (minimum 0.488 ms, maximum 1.851 ms). The two small timing samples are not a scaling comparison; host scheduling and overhead dominate at this duration. They establish measured cached online cost separately from expensive scientific construction.

The literature designs were inspected in `literature/ape-apv-free-surface-applications/wvm-v5-examples/CheckWeakThermalSeasonalDamping.m` and `CompareWeakThermalDampingContinuations.m`. Their default prepared inputs are absent at `literature/ape-apv-free-surface-applications/wvm-v5-examples/output/weak-thermal-damping-prepared-64-513.nc` and `.../weak-thermal-damping-full-01-100x.nc`; mirrored 23.8 MB and 144.5 MB files exist under `literature/ape-apv-free-surface/wvm-v5-examples/output/`. No old checkpoint was reinterpreted or changed. Fresh bounded controls reuse the exact same-space seasonal reference and matched-continuation comparison design. The original historical operator variants, pins and scientific claims remain separate.
